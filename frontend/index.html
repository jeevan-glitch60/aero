    HEAVY FUEL // CI

    Four-Stroke Heavy-Fuel Compression-Ignition Engine

    Four-stroke compression-ignition engine designed to operate with heavy fuels such as diesel or aviation kerosene-class fuels in this synthetic demonstrator.

    KEY STRENGTHS:
    • Strong fuel efficiency for long endurance
    • Simplified military fuel logistics concept
    • Lower fuel volatility relative to gasoline
    KEY TRADE-OFFS:
    • Higher engine mass
    • Higher compression and structural load
    • Thermal and vibration management remain critical
    ↔️
    BOXER // OPPOSED

    Horizontally Opposed / Boxer Reciprocating-Piston Engine

    Reciprocating engine with cylinders arranged horizontally opposite each other around a central crankshaft.

    KEY STRENGTHS:
    • Low engine profile fits slender airframes
    • Good primary balance (opposed pistons)
    • Suitable geometry for streamlined installation
    KEY TRADE-OFFS:
    • Wider engine package
    • Cowl and cooling-path design are important
    • Cylinder temperature imbalance must be monitored
    🔺
    ROTARY IC // EPITROCHOID

    Wankel Rotary Internal-Combustion Engine

    Note: Rotary internal-combustion architecture; included for propulsion-system comparison.

    Rotary internal-combustion engine using a triangular rotor moving inside an epitrochoid-like housing rather than conventional pistons.

    KEY STRENGTHS:
    • Compact architecture & low frontal area
    • High power-to-weight potential
    • Smooth operation & ultra-low reciprocating vibration
    KEY TRADE-OFFS:
    • Thermal management challenge in hot arc
    • Apex seal wear/degradation concern
    • Fuel efficiency differs from piston engines
    🌀
    FORCED INDUCTION // TURBO

    Turbocharged / Supercharged Multi-Cylinder Inline or V Engine

    Multi-cylinder inline or V-style engine with forced induction to sustain intake pressure and available power at altitude.

    KEY STRENGTHS:

    • Adjustable boost & manifold-pressure control
    KEY TRADE-OFFS:
    • Added turbocharger/supercharger complexity
    • Additional thermal load & heat rejection
    • More failure modes in boost, lube & air-path
